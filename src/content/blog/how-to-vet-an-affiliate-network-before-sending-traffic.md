You are about to spend your own money sending traffic to somebody else's offer. Before you build a single ad, spend thirty minutes checking whether the network deserves it. Here are the nine questions we would ask, with the answers that should end the conversation.

## 1. Who are they, legally?

Find the legal entity, the address, and the people. A network that hides all three is asking you to trust a logo. Check the footer of the site, the terms of use, and LinkedIn. If the founders have a track record you can verify, that is worth more than any testimonial.

**Walk away if:** no entity name, no address, nobody with a face.

## 2. How did they approve you?

Instant approval feels good and means nothing. A network that does not verify you is not verifying anyone else either, which means the offer you are about to build on is exposed to every kind of traffic. Those offers get pulled.

**Walk away if:** you got a login before anyone asked where your traffic comes from.

## 3. Can you identify the advertiser?

You should know which brand you are promoting, that it exists, and that it has the licenses it needs. In telehealth, that includes licensed providers and, for prescription products, LegitScript certification where required. If the network will not tell you who the advertiser is, you cannot check any of it.

**Walk away if:** the offer is "a leading GLP-1 brand" with no name.

## 4. Are the terms in writing before launch?

Payout, qualifying event, cap, allowed sources, banned sources, approved claims, reversal rules, payment schedule. All of it, before the first click. A verbal "we pay $300" is not a term.

**Walk away if:** anything on that list is "we will figure it out."

## 5. Can you verify tracking yourself?

Ask for a test link and fire a test conversion. It should show up in your dashboard with your sub IDs intact. If the network cannot prove the postback works before launch, it will not be able to prove a sale happened after.

**Walk away if:** "just trust the numbers."

## 6. What happens when a sale is reversed?

Refunds, chargebacks, duplicates, and fraud all get reversed. The question is how, when, and whether you can see why. A good network shows you the reversal with a reason. A bad one just shows you a smaller number.

**Walk away if:** reversals are a black box.

## 7. When do you get paid, and by whom?

Net 30 is normal. Net 15 or weekly for proven partners is a good sign. Payment that depends on "when the advertiser pays us" is a warning, because it means the network is not carrying any risk.

**Walk away if:** the payment schedule is not written down.

## 8. What are the claim rules?

For health offers, you need the approved claim set per offer, and you need to know who owns those claims. It should be the brand. If a network hands you a swipe file full of claims nobody has reviewed, the offer is a liability, and so is your account.

**Walk away if:** "just do not say cure."

## 9. What happens when you have a problem?

Tracking breaks. Landers go down. Ads get rejected. Ask who answers, and how fast. Then test it by sending one real question before you launch and see how long the reply takes.

**Walk away if:** the reply comes from a generic inbox three days later.

## The pattern

Every question above comes down to one thing: does this network verify, track, and pay in a way you can check yourself? If the answer is yes, build. If it is "trust us," find another network.
