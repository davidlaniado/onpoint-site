"We only accept compliant traffic." Every telehealth network says it. Very few can tell you what it means. Here is the definition we actually use, and the checks behind it.

## The four-part definition

Traffic is compliant when all four of these are true:

1. **The source is disclosed and verified.** The partner has told the brand where the traffic comes from (Meta, search, native, email, SMS, a community) and can prove it. Undisclosed sources are not compliant, even if they convert.
2. **The creative uses only approved claims.** Every statement about the treatment, the results, the pricing, and the process comes from a claim set the brand has reviewed. The brand owns the claims. The partner does not invent them.
3. **The funnel is one the brand controls.** The patient lands on a page the brand has approved, goes through the brand's intake, and is treated by the brand's licensed providers. No partner-built checkout pretending to be the brand.
4. **Tracking is auditable.** Conversions are recorded through a postback or pixel the brand can see, with the partner and channel attached. If the brand cannot tie a sale to a source, it cannot verify the first three points.

If any one of these fails, the traffic is not compliant. Conversion rate does not enter into it.

## Why this matters more in health than anywhere else

In most affiliate categories, a bad partner costs you money. In telehealth, a bad partner can cost you the business.

- **Ad platforms** hold the advertiser accountable for how its offer is promoted. Unapproved claims from a partner can get the brand's own accounts restricted.
- **Payment processors** watch chargeback and refund rates. Misleading creative drives both.
- **Certification bodies** like LegitScript review how prescription products are marketed. Losing certification can shut down paid channels overnight.
- **Regulators** do not distinguish between "the brand said it" and "an affiliate said it." The claim ran under the brand's offer.

That is why the definition above is strict. It is not about being difficult. It is about the brand still existing next year.

## The checklist we run before a partner goes live

**Identity.** Real business, real person, verifiable. KYC is not optional.

**Traffic source.** Declared in writing. If a partner says Meta, we expect to see Meta. If a partner says "various," the answer is no.

**Channel and placement approval.** Some offers allow SMS, some do not. Some allow native, some only search. Each offer has its own list, and partners get it before launch.

**Claim set.** Every offer comes with the claims the brand has approved and the claims that are off limits. Partners build inside that box.

**Creative review.** Ads and landing pages are reviewed before launch for advertising standards. We are not reviewing medical accuracy. That stays with the brand. We are checking the creative uses the approved claims and nothing else.

**Tracking.** Server-to-server postback confirmed, test conversion fired, partner and channel visible in the report.

## What partners get out of this

Strict rules sound like friction until you have watched an open network burn an offer in six weeks. Compliant traffic is what keeps a payout stable for a year instead of a month. Partners who run clean get offers that last, caps that grow, and a brand that answers their emails.
