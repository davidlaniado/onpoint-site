Here is the rule that matters most in a telehealth affiliate program, and the one most networks quietly skip: **the brand owns every health claim.** Not the network. Not the partner. The brand.

## Why the rule exists

When an affiliate ad says a treatment does something, the regulator, the ad platform, and the payment processor all see one advertiser: the brand whose offer it was. "An affiliate wrote that" has never been a defense.

That means every claim running under your offer is your claim. If you did not approve it, you are carrying liability for a sentence you have never read.

## What "owning the claims" looks like in practice

**You write the claim set.** Before the offer launches, you produce a document that says what partners may state about the treatment, the results, the process, the providers, and the pricing. You also list what is off limits. Your clinical and legal people sign off.

**Partners build inside it.** Every ad, every landing page, every email a partner creates uses claims from the set. They can be creative with the angle, the format, the story. They cannot be creative with the medicine.

**The network enforces it, and only that.** A good network reviews creative before launch and checks it against your claim set. It does not add claims, soften claims, or "improve" them. If a partner wants to say something new, the request goes to you.

**Violations have a consequence.** A partner running unapproved claims gets paused. The traffic source gets removed. You hear about it from the network before you hear about it from a platform.

## What goes wrong when brands skip this

- **The swipe file problem.** The network hands partners a folder of "proven" ads from other offers. Half of them make claims your product cannot support. They run anyway.
- **The 2am problem.** A partner launches a new angle on Friday night. It gets 50,000 impressions before anyone at your company sees it. Someone screenshots it.
- **The account problem.** The ad platform flags the claim. It does not flag the partner. It flags you, and your own campaigns go down with it.
- **The certification problem.** A certification body reviews how your prescription product is being marketed. The unapproved affiliate ad is what they find.

None of these are hypothetical. They are the normal failure mode of open networks in health.

## What this rule does not mean

It does not mean partners cannot be creative. The best-performing health ads are rarely the ones with the boldest claim. They are the ones with the sharpest angle, the most honest story, and a claim set used well.

It does not mean the network is useless on compliance. Reviewing creative against a claim set is real work, and it catches most problems before they run. It just means the network is checking your rules, not writing them.
