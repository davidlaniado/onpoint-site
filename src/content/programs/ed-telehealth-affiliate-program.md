## What the offer is

Telehealth programs for erectile dysfunction. The patient completes a short intake, a licensed provider reviews it, and approved patients receive generic sildenafil or tadalafil, or a chewable or fast-dissolve format, on a subscription. Entry pricing is designed to convert cold traffic; lifetime value comes from renewals.

## Who can run it

Verified media partners approved for the offer's channels. ED is the category where ad platforms reject the most creative, so partners need to be comfortable working inside a tight claim set. Partners who treat that as a craft, not a limitation, do very well here.

Brands with an ED program can apply to distribute through the network.

## What partners get

- A platform-safe claim set that tells you what has been approved and what gets ads rejected.
- Approved landing pages, including pre-sell pages for cold traffic where the offer supports them.
- Server-to-server postbacks on the charged order and funnel event visibility.
- A partner team that buys ED media itself and can tell you what is currently getting through review.
- Payment on approved performance under agreed terms.

## What converts

ED is a story category. The creative that scales on the network is relatable and a little wry: a wife's point of view, a "read the comments" style reply video, a plain product-on-shelf shot with an honest price. Explicit content does not scale because it does not get approved. Long-form static "advertorial" images with a first-person story have been a consistent performer. Video with a strong first three seconds and a clear price wins on cold traffic.

## What is off limits

- Explicit language or imagery. It fails platform review and it fails our review.
- Performance guarantees or claims about duration, size, or partner satisfaction.
- Naming competitor brands or using their pages.
- Undisclosed traffic sources or borrowed ad accounts.
