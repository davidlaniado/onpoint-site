## The definition

Most networks say "compliant traffic" and mean "we asked nicely." On OnPoint it means four specific things, and all four have to be true:

1. **Disclosed, verified source.** The partner has told us where the traffic comes from and it matches what tracking shows.
2. **Approved claims only.** Every statement in the creative comes from the brand's claim set.
3. **Brand-controlled funnel.** The patient lands on your page, goes through your intake, and is treated by your providers.
4. **Auditable tracking.** The conversion is recorded through a postback the brand can see, with the partner and channel attached.

## How it is enforced before launch

- Identity, KYC, channel, and traffic source verification for every partner.
- Channel and placement approval per offer.
- Creative and funnel review against the claim set.
- Tracking test with a real click and a real conversion before any spend.

## How it is enforced after launch

- Traffic source monitoring against declared sources.
- Ongoing creative review as partners add angles.
- Refund, chargeback, and quality monitoring by partner.
- Removal of sources that fall below standard.

## Why it matters more in health

In most categories a bad partner costs money. In telehealth a bad partner can cost the brand its ad accounts, its merchant accounts, or its certification. That is why access is gated, and why we would rather turn a partner away than explain to a brand what ran under its name.
